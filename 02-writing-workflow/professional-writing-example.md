# Professional Writing Example — Deployment Delay Notice

**Scenario:** As the Expert Technician on a project, a client-facing deployment deadline has shifted two weeks (May 30 → June 13) due to a vendor-caused delay. A 150-word, 3-paragraph email needs to go to a 6-person technical team.

## Step 1 — AI-Generated Draft

> Subject: Updated Project Deadline – New Target: June 13
>
> Hi team,
>
> I want to give you a quick update on our timeline. Our client-facing deadline has moved from May 30 to June 13, a two-week shift caused by a delay on the vendor's end that was outside our control. The client has been informed and is aligned with the new date.
>
> This shift gives us some breathing room, but it doesn't mean we slow down. We'll use the extra time to strengthen quality and close any gaps in our current work, rather than treating it as a pause. Our priorities and task order remain the same for now.
>
> Here's what I need from each of you: please review your current tasks by end of day tomorrow and flag anything that's blocked or at risk under the new timeline. Once I have that picture, I'll share an updated plan for the team.
>
> Thanks for your continued focus and flexibility.
>
> Best,
> [Name]

## Step 2 — Fact Verification

Checked against the source facts before anything else:
- ✅ Old date (May 30) and new date (June 13) — correct, exactly a 2-week shift.
- ✅ Cause stated as "vendor delay outside our control" — matches, no invented detail added.
- ✅ Client-informed status — stated accurately, not overstated as "client is happy."
- ✅ No fabricated numbers, names, or commitments were added beyond what was provided.

## Step 3 — Tone Refinement

- First pass came in at ~165 words against a 150-word target — trimmed filler phrasing ("Quick update on our timeline" shortened, redundant transition removed) without cutting any required fact.
- Confirmed tone stayed calm and solution-focused rather than apologetic — no over-explaining or excessive reassurance, per the "state the delay plainly, without over-apologizing" principle.

## Step 4 — Human Review / Sign-off

- Confirmed the email contains exactly **one** clear next action (reviewing tasks and flagging blockers by end of day tomorrow) rather than several competing asks.
- Confirmed length: exactly 150 words, 3 paragraphs, no jargon — matching the original brief.
- Approved for sending under my name as the accountable sender.
