# Prompt Library — Technical Expert

A collection of reusable, structured prompts built during L0-FGP and applied to real recurring tasks in the Technical Expert role: incident communication, technical status reporting, resource/infrastructure updates, and root-cause documentation.

| Prompt Name | Workplace Task | Framework | Prompt | Expected Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deployment Delay Notice** | Informing the team that a client-facing deployment has shifted due to a vendor-caused delay | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* Deadline moved 2 weeks (May 30 → June 13) due to a vendor delay outside our control. *Action:* Write a 150-word, 3-paragraph email to a 6-person technical team. *Role:* Calm, direct Project/Technical Lead. *Expected Output:* Calm, professional, solution-focused tone; states new deadline + reason; one clear next action; no jargon. | A 150-word, 3-paragraph email stating the new deadline (June 13), the vendor-delay cause, and a single next action: review current tasks and flag anything blocked by end of day tomorrow. |
| **Infrastructure Budget Cut — Peer Update** | Giving a peer engineer a quick, informal heads-up about a cloud infrastructure budget cut | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* Cloud infrastructure budget reduced 12% for Q3. *Action:* Write an informal, direct heads-up to a peer engineer. *Role:* Technical colleague. *Expected Output:* Short, casual message, awareness only, no action items. | A short message: "Heads up — infra budget's down 12% this quarter, heard it in the ops sync. Wanted you to have it before it's official." |
| **Infrastructure Budget Cut — Manager Update** | Escalating the same infrastructure budget cut to a manager with mitigation options | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* Same 12% infrastructure budget cut. *Action:* Write a formal, solution-oriented update. *Role:* Technical Expert reporting to a manager. *Expected Output:* States the cut, then two concrete mitigation options (defer non-critical infra work vs. reallocate existing capacity), and asks for a decision. | A formal email presenting the 12% cut and two clearly labeled mitigation options, ending with a request for a decision. |
| **Infrastructure Budget Cut — Client Update** | Communicating the same cut to an external client without exposing internal details | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* Same 12% cut, but may affect delivery scope. *Action:* Write a formal, diplomatic update. *Role:* Technical Expert representing the team externally. *Expected Output:* Transparent about the change and its potential scope impact, reassures on quality, promises a follow-up plan. | A short, diplomatic email acknowledging the budget adjustment, its potential impact on scope, and a commitment to share an updated plan within days. |
| **Root-Cause Summary (R.C.T.O.)** | Writing a technical root-cause summary after resolving a production incident | R.C.T.O. | *Role:* "You are a senior technical expert writing a post-incident summary." *Context:* "A checkout-flow bug affected 40% of users for several hours before being patched." *Task:* "Summarize the root cause, the fix applied, and the remaining open risk." *Output Format:* "3 short paragraphs: Root Cause, Fix Applied, Remaining Risk — no more than 150 words total." | A concise 3-part summary usable directly in an incident report, with the remaining open risk (e.g., a second unresolved bug) clearly flagged rather than omitted. |
| **Recurring Status Update (Few-Shot)** | Producing incident/status updates that always match the team's expected format | Few-Shot | "Write a status update using this exact format: `Status: [state] — Impact: [who/what] — Next Check-in: [time]`. Example: `Status: Monitoring — Impact: No user-facing impact — Next check-in: 2 PM.` Now write one for: database latency spike, affecting the reporting dashboard only, next check-in in 1 hour." | `Status: Investigating — Impact: Reporting dashboard only, no core service impact — Next check-in: 1 hour.` — matches house format exactly instead of a generic paragraph. |
| **Executive Brief From Scattered Updates** | Turning three days of scattered engineering/marketing updates into a one-minute executive brief | C.A.R.E. | *Context:* Three days of scattered project updates (deployment status, an engineering capacity risk, an unanswered client pricing question, an unconfirmed budget concern). *Action:* Summarize as an executive brief. *Role:* Technical lead writing to a director. *Expected Output:* Opens with a To/From/Subject header, leads with the bottom line, 3–5 bullets, flags anything unconfirmed as unconfirmed rather than stating it as fact. | A one-minute brief opening with "Bottom line: full rollout is at risk pending a capacity decision Thursday," followed by 4 labeled points, with the unconfirmed budget item explicitly marked "Unconfirmed" rather than stated as settled. |
| **Decisions & Actions Extraction** | Extracting a clean action-item table from raw, unstructured meeting notes | C.A.R.E. | "Extract every action item and decision from the notes below into a 5-column table: Action Item / Owner / Deadline / Priority / Status. If an owner or deadline isn't stated in the source, write `[Not Specified]` instead of guessing." | A 6-row table where 3 of 6 rows correctly show `[Not Specified]` for owner or deadline, instead of an invented name or date. |

## Notes
- All prompts are tied to the same Technical Expert scenario: supporting a technical rollout, its risks, and its stakeholders.
- Prompts 2–4 show the same underlying fact (a 12% infrastructure budget cut) communicated in three different tones for three different audiences — the fact never changes, only the delivery does.
- The extraction prompt enforces a "never invent, always flag" rule that is reused again in Section 3 and Section 5.
